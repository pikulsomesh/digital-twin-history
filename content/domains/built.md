## The short version

Buildings and cities moved from drawings to objects to live, sensor-connected models. City twins are among the most visible twins, and they connect most directly to the 1991 vision of [*Mirror Worlds*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_Worlds).

## Then

- **From lines to objects (1970s–1990s).** Charles Eastman's Building Description System laid foundations for [building information modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_information_modeling) (BIM), in which walls and systems carry data, not just geometry.
- **The first virtual cities (1996–1998).** Helsinki Arena 2000 and Digital City Kyoto connected virtual urban spaces with real places and data. The Heathrow Express project linked movement sensors to a digital model during construction ([Digital twin: History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_twin)).
- **City data standards (2008).** [CityGML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityGML) gave cities a shared, semantic way to describe 3D environments.
- **National city twins (2014 onward).** [Virtual Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_Singapore) built a semantic 3D model of an entire city-state for planning and simulation.

## Now

- **Building twins** combine BIM with building management systems and occupancy sensors to manage energy, comfort, space and maintenance.
- **Infrastructure twins** of bridges, tunnels, rail and water networks support inspection and structural health monitoring.
- **City twins** support planning (sunlight, wind, views, flood risk), mobility and emergency response.
- **Construction twins** compare as-built scans with the design to catch deviations early.

## What's next

Faster capture from drones and phones ([Gaussian splatting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04079)), city twins linked to climate twins, and stronger privacy rules for data about people in public space.
