## The short version

Energy companies had live data and control rooms decades before most industries. That made power plants, grids, pipelines and wind farms natural homes for operational twins focused on reliability, efficiency and safety.

## Then

- **Control rooms and mimic panels (1960s–1980s).** [SCADA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCADA) collected telemetry from remote sites into a central room, where the network was drawn on a mimic panel with live lamps: an analogue operational twin.
- **Plant simulators.** Power plants, especially nuclear ones, used full-scope simulators replicating the control room and plant behavior to train operators.
- **Common data interfaces (1990s–2000s).** [OPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Platform_Communications) and [OPC UA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPC_Unified_Architecture) made it easier to bring data from many suppliers' equipment into one model.

## Now

- **Asset twins** of turbines, transformers, compressors and pumps for condition monitoring and predictive maintenance.
- **Wind and solar farm twins** that optimize output and plan maintenance against weather forecasts.
- **Grid twins** that model power flows and test how the network responds to new renewables, storage, electric vehicles and extreme weather.
- **Process twins** in oil, gas and chemicals for optimization and safety.

## What's next

Grid twins coupled to weather and climate twins, machine-learned surrogates for faster planning studies, and twins that coordinate millions of distributed devices.
