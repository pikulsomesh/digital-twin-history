## The short version

People have been "twinned", in a simple sense, for as long as there have been time clocks and rosters. Today the people layer of a twin covers workforce, skills, safety and human-machine collaboration, and the wider idea of a twin of the whole organization. It is also where privacy and ethics matter most.

## Then

- **Attendance records (1888 onward).** Mechanical [time clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_clock) created a continuous record of who was at work: one of the oldest data streams in any factory.
- **The visible line (1950s–1960s).** [Andon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andon_(manufacturing)) systems let any worker signal a problem, so the line's state reflected what people on the floor saw.
- **Execution records (1990s).** [MES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_execution_system) systems recorded which operator performed which step on which unit.
- **Mirror Worlds (1991).** Gelernter imagined software mirrors of whole organizations, not just places ([Mirror Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_Worlds)).

## Now

- **Workforce and skills planning:** matching people, shifts and qualifications to demand.
- **Ergonomics and safety:** simulating tasks to reduce strain and risk before a line is built.
- **Training:** immersive twins let new operators practice on a virtual machine first. This is the [Link Trainer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Trainer) idea, applied to the factory.
- **Standards:** [ISO 23247](https://www.iso.org/standard/75066.html) explicitly lists *personnel* as an observable manufacturing element.
- **Twins of the organization:** models of business processes and decisions, popularized from the late 2010s.

## What's next

Human-AI teaming in which agents work through a twin and people approve, and clear rules on what data about workers a twin may hold.
