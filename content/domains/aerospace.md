## The short version

Aerospace gave the digital twin its name and its first formal definition. Its central idea, a virtual counterpart that follows one specific vehicle through its whole life, came from the need to keep spacecraft and aircraft safe when you can't easily inspect them.

## Then

- **Simulators as stand-ins (1929–1960s).** From the [Link Trainer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_Trainer) to NASA's mission simulators, aerospace learned to rehearse reality on a faithful copy.
- **Apollo 13 (1970).** Ground simulators, updated with flight data, were used to work out how to bring a crippled spacecraft home ([Apollo 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_13)). It is widely cited as the first twin in practice.
- **Physics-based analysis.** NASA's [NASTRAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastran) (1968) and the [Kalman filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalman_filter), used in Apollo navigation, supplied the simulation and data-fusion foundations.
- **Naming and definition (2010–2012).** NASA's 2010 technology roadmapping introduced the term. In 2011 the US Air Force proposed an airframe twin for structural life ([Tuegel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/154798)), and in 2012 NASA and the Air Force defined the twin as a probabilistic simulation that mirrors "the life of its corresponding flying twin" ([Glaessgen & Stargel](https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2012-1818)).

## Now

- **Engine and airframe fleet twins:** each engine's operating history drives individualized maintenance and remaining-life estimates.
- **Digital engineering:** defense and space programs use connected, authoritative models and a *digital thread* from requirements through sustainment.
- **Mission twins:** spacecraft operations teams keep twins of vehicles in flight to test commands before sending them.
- **Shared definition:** the [AIAA & AIA position paper](https://www.aia-aerospace.org/wp-content/uploads/Digital-Twin-Institute-Position-Paper-December-2020-1.pdf) (2020) set out definitions and a value framework widely used across the industry.

## What's next

Certification credit for twin-based evidence, uncertainty-aware prognostics, and autonomy tested in simulation before flight.
