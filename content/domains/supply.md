## The short version

Supply-chain twins model flows of goods, vehicles and information across warehouses, ports, airports and networks. Their roots are in operations research and simulation, accelerated by tagging, tracking and real-time visibility.

## Then

- **Simulation and operations research (1950s onward).** Discrete-event and [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_method) simulation let planners test network and inventory decisions on paper and then on computers.
- **Tagging things (1999).** The phrase "[Internet of Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_things)" was coined while promoting RFID in supply chains, the start of products that report their own location.
- **Infrastructure models (1990s).** Airport and rail projects linked sensors to digital models ([Digital twin: History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_twin)).

## Now

- **Network twins** for planning capacity, inventory and resilience under disruption.
- **Warehouse twins** that plan layouts and orchestrate fleets of mobile robots.
- **Port and airport twins** that forecast congestion and coordinate operations.

## What's next

Warehouses and yards as training grounds for [Physical AI](#/frontier), and twins shared across companies without exposing sensitive data.
