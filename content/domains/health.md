## The short version

Health care is moving from *virtual anatomy* to *virtual patients*. The promise is a personalized twin that helps choose treatments and test devices and drugs *in silico*. The bar for validation, privacy and regulation is higher here than in any other field.

## Then

- **Digital anatomy (1994–1995).** The [Visible Human Project](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/research/visible/visible_human.html) created a complete digital record of human anatomy.
- **Virtual patients for regulators (2008).** A type 1 diabetes simulator with a population of virtual patients was accepted by the US FDA as a substitute for animal trials in pre-clinical testing of control algorithms ([UVA/Padova simulator](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37743740/)).
- **Organ twins (2014 onward).** Multi-physics heart models brought anatomy, electrophysiology, mechanics and blood flow together for device testing ([research landscape](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11344555/)).

## Now

- **Device and drug testing in silico** on virtual patient populations.
- **Patient-specific models** for planning procedures, such as cardiac and orthopedic surgery.
- **Hospital operations twins** for beds, theaters, staffing and patient flow.
- **Research agenda:** the [National Academies](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/26894/chapter/4) (2023) used biomedicine as a core case study and stressed VVUQ, ethics and privacy.

## What's next

Europe's [Virtual Human Twins](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/virtual-human-twins-launch-european-virtual-human-twins-initiative) initiative aims at an ecosystem of validated, combinable models of organs, diseases and patients. Open questions include consent, data ownership and how regulators will judge AI-based models.
